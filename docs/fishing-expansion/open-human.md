## Which rolled copy `take:` parts with, if it may part with one at all

`take: 1 <item>` spends a stacked copy or the worn one and cannot reach a **rolled copy sitting
in the pack**. Every item carrying an `item-level:` is rolled, so this is every piece of gear
in the game: a player who owns two nets and wears neither cannot be asked to hand one over.
`take: worn <slot>` was added on 2026-09-03 and reaches the worn one, which is why a mishap can
cost a player their line; nothing reaches the spare.

The reason it is a ruling rather than a fix is that the copies are not interchangeable. Two
small fishing nets have different rolls, different planes, and possibly a jewel socketed into
one of them. So `take: 1 small-fishing-net` has to say *which*, and the candidates each mean
something different in play:

- the worst one by its own roll, which is what a person hands a boy and what a player would
  expect
- the first in the pack, which is cheap and arbitrary and will one day take somebody's build
- refuse it always, and make every such beat name a slot — which is where the language stands
  today, and which means an NPC can only ever be given the thing in your hand

The Rook beat is written round it with `take: worn mainhand` for now, so nothing is blocked.

*Moves when: he says whether `take:` may spend a rolled copy from the pack, and by what rule it
picks one. The first two are then a lane's afternoon; the third is a line on the page saying so
and closes the `open-agent.md` line beside it.*

## Whether the top band is meant to take 1.43x the curve, or the mire is

`top-floor-to-30` walks the shipped world from character creation to level 30 in **626
game-minutes against the 437 the declared curve budgets**, and the whole of the overrun is in
the stretch above 20: 458 minutes where the curve gives 250.

The reason is measured and is not the water — the perch and the carp read their ceilings
exactly, and the tench is five percent under only because of the same thing. It is the swamp
mire: the bog-lurkers respawn every five minutes and are refought across a climb that takes
hours, and the bread paste the tench takes is only sold on Market Row, which is a long walk.

Three answers, and which one is wanted is a judgement about what the mire is for:

- it is right as it stands — the mire is meant to be dear, and a floor route walked by someone
  who never fights better than the bare minimum is meant to read slow
- the mire's waters should pay for the fight, which means sweeping them without `unkillable`
  and raising the tench and the eels to what that reads
- the walk is the problem rather than the fight, and bread paste wants a seller nearer the water
  — Hob is already out there and already sells things

For scale: thieving's shipped floors span 0.78x to 2.08x across the same band, and its geared
route reaches 0.92x by carrying better kit than this one does. So 1.43x is inside the range the
world already ships, and is being raised because it is now known rather than because it is
plainly wrong.

*Moves when: he says which of the three the mire is, or that 1.43x is the answer. The first two
are then a lane's afternoon with a sweep in it.*

## Whether the fishing contest's five minutes is a fair window

The match is on while a buff the weigh-master gives lasts, and the design doc sets that at five
minutes of game time. Nothing has walked it at a level where landing a fish worth weighing is
uncertain: the routes that prove it stand on saves handed the gear. Whether five minutes is
tense or trivial is a question play answers.

*Moves when: a speedrun or a play session says how many casts five minutes buys at the contest's
own band, and he says whether that is the match he wants.*
